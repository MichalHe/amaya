## Writing conventions (reports, design documents, papers):
Use strictly technical language.
- No metaphor, analogy, or figurative language ("pay-off", "earns its place", "attacks", "in anger").
- No evaluative or promotional adjectives ("aggressive", "powerful", "elegant", "most important").
  State the property, not a judgement of it.
- Declarative statements of fact: "Pass X matches Y and produces Z", not "Pass X is designed to help
  with Y".
- Attribute every claim. Behaviour cites the source as `path:symbol` (no line numbers - they drift).
  Numbers cite their provenance and how to regenerate them.
- Separate what is measured from what is not. Never imply a measurement that was not made; include an
  explicit section listing what remains unquantified.
- No rhetorical structure: no motivating narrative, no "the interesting part is". Use tables and
  enumerated lists wherever the content is enumerable.
- Mark status at the point of description: disabled by default, incomplete, experimental.
- Use long descriptive identifiers in prose, as in code.
